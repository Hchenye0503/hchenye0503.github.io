Project question

How might a robot pet respond to a situation differently depending on what it perceives and its own internal state? This MuJoCo prototype combines VLM-derived human behavioral cues with simulated internal drives to guide an LLM planner.

All demonstrations and evaluations are in simulation.

▶Watch the simulation demo ↗Three-minute project video on YouTube

What I developed

  • Integrated visual cues and simulated internal states into LLM-based behavior selection.
  • Designed speed-dependent rewards for PPO locomotion to address standing-still behavior under low-speed commands.
  • Evaluated velocity tracking and stability across three training seeds.

The project builds on Pollen Robotics’ Microduck model and an existing training environment. The repository documents the upstream components and project-specific work.

From cues to behavior

  1. Perceive. Use visual observations to describe human behavior and the surrounding situation.
  2. Plan. Combine those cues with simulated internal states to select pet-like behaviors.
  3. Execute. Validate the plan and use motion policies to carry it out in MuJoCo.

The internal states are designed variables of the simulated robot. Visual cues are inputs to the interaction logic.

Evaluation & limitations

Low-speed walking exposed a reward-design problem: standing still could remain attractive under small velocity commands. I designed speed-dependent rewards and evaluated the resulting policies across three training seeds.

The selected policy passed the main gait criteria; the other seeds did not pass all of them. Straight-line drift and slow starts under a lower simulated voltage remain limitations. The repository provides the evaluation conditions and distinguishes development tests from held-out runs.
