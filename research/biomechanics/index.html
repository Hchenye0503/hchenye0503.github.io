#Biomechanics#MotionAnalysis

Small-joint biomechanics

A controlled motion platform for measuring how structural changes alter joint mechanics.

SAM Lab, National Taiwan University
Prof. Dian-Ru Li
Jan 2024–Oct 2025
Journal article, 2026
Research questionMy contributionMeasurement approachOutcome & scopePublication

Research question

How can an experimental platform detect altered mechanical responses under controlled small-joint defect conditions? This project paired repeatable limb motion with synchronized force measurements and optical tracking.

My contribution

I designed the cadaveric limb motion platform for synchronized force measurement and optical tracking. Working with my co-first author, I used PCA and k-means clustering to group marker trajectories and apparent in-plane strain analysis to quantify regional deformation. I also led manuscript writing and figure preparation.

The broader study was a collaboration at NTU involving engineering and orthopedic researchers.

Measurement approach

  1. Control the motion. Apply repeatable flexion–extension to compare responses under defined structural conditions.
  2. Synchronize measurements. Record reaction force alongside optical marker trajectories.
  3. Characterize deformation. Group trajectories and estimate regional apparent in-plane strain.

Outcome & scope

The published study found that global force and regional strain provide complementary, motion-dependent information about mechanically altered joints. The platform offers a controlled framework for investigating such changes.

This was a proof-of-concept study using ex vivo specimens. Its contribution is an experimental evaluation approach, with future applications to small-joint implant research.

Publication

C.-Y. Hong†, C.-H. Chou†, Y.-J. Wang, K.-L. Wu, S.-J. Yang, C.-H. Chang, and D.-R. Li. “An experimental cadaveric limb motion platform for detecting altered mechanical responses under small-joint defect conditions.” Medical Engineering & Physics, 147(9), 095006, 2026.
